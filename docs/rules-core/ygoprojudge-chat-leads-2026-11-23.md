## Q01

**Question.** TCG ruling question. Can a player activate Pot of Greed ("Draw 2 cards.") when they have only 1 card left in their Deck, or no cards? If they can, what happens? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** A player cannot activate "Pot of Greed" if they have 1 or 0 cards remaining in their Deck. 

## Q02

Can this effect be activated if the player has no Level 4 or lower Normal Monster in their hand, or if the zone it points to is occupied? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** No, you cannot activate the effect of "Link Spider" in either of those situations. Under fundamental game rules, you must be able to fully resolve all mandatory parts of an effect at the time of activation. ## Q03

**Question.** TCG ruling question. A card effect targets a monster on the field. In response, the targeted monster is banished and then returned to the field (for example by a flicker effect) before the targeting effect resolves. When the targeting effect resolves, is the returned monster still treated as "that target", or is it a new card so the effect does not apply to it? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** When a monster leaves the field and returns, game mechanics treat it as a completely new card. Therefore, it is no longer recognized as the original target, and the targeting effect will not apply to it when it resolves. Because no specific cards or official database snippets were provided in the context, this is based on general rules knowledge; always remember that the on-site Head Judge is the final authority. 

## Q04

**Question.** TCG ruling question. A Normal Spell Card is activated as Chain Link 1, and the opponent chains a card as Chain Link 2. When Chain Link 1 resolves, does the Normal Spell go to the Graveyard immediately after its own effect resolves, or only after the whole chain has finished resolving? Does it matter for cards that care about cards on the field during the chain? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** A Normal Spell Card remains on the field until the entire chain has finished resolving; it is not sent to the Graveyard immediately after its own Chain Link resolves. Because it stays on the field during the chain's resolution, it will be counted by any card effect that checks the number of cards on the field. As no specific card data was provided in the context, this ruling is based on general game mechanics; the on-site Head Judge is always authoritative. 

## Q05

**Question.** TCG ruling question. A trigger effect is worded "If a monster is sent to the GY: You can ..." (not "each time"). During one chain, two separate monsters are sent to the GY at two different times (by two different Chain Links). After the chain resolves, can that trigger effect be activated once or twice? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** The trigger effect can only be activated once. Standard "If...:" Trigger effects do not queue multiple activations if their condition is met multiple times during a single chain; they only check if the event occurred at least once. 

## Q06

**Question.** TCG ruling question. Can a monster's ATK or DEF go below 0? For example, a monster with 500 ATK loses 1000 ATK, and then gains 700 ATK: is its ATK 200 or 0? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** A monster's ATK or DEF can never drop below 0, but the game continuously tracks the total sum of all ATK/DEF modifiers applied to it. Because the game calculates the net total of all gains and losses against the base stat, your monster's final ATK will be 200. 

## Q07

**Question.** TCG ruling question. A monster's Level was changed by a card effect (for example from 4 to 5). When it is used as Synchro Material or Xyz Material, is its current (changed) Level used, or its original Level? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** When a monster's Level is changed by a card effect, its current (changed) Level is used when it is used as Synchro or Xyz Material. You do not use its original Level unless a specific card effect explicitly tells you to do so. 

## Q08

**Question.** TCG ruling question. During the Damage Step, before damage calculation, the attacking monster or the attack target leaves the field (for example it is destroyed or banished by a card effect). What happens to the attack: is there any damage calculation, does the remaining monster count as having battled, and does the game proceed to the end of the Damage Step? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** If either the attacking or defending monster leaves the field during the Damage Step before damage calculation, damage calculation is skipped. The remaining monster is not considered to have "battled," and the game proceeds directly to the End of the Damage Step. *(Note: Because no official database context was provided for this query, this ruling is based on general rules knowledge. ## Q09

**Question.** TCG ruling question. During the Damage Step, before damage calculation, a card effect changes the attacking monster to Defense Position. Is the attack stopped, or does damage calculation still happen, and if it happens, which value of the attacker is used? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** Because no official database context was provided for this scenario, this ruling relies on general Yu-Gi-Oh! TCG mechanics; always defer to your on-site Head Judge. If an attacking monster is changed to Defense Position during the Damage Step (before damage calculation), the attack is **not** stopped and damage calculation still occurs. The attacking monster will use its **DEF** for damage calculation. 

## Q10

**Question.** TCG ruling question. A monster declares an attack. Later in the same Battle Phase it is changed to face-down Defense Position and then flipped face-up in Attack Position again by card effects. Can it declare another attack that turn? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** No, the monster cannot declare another attack during that turn. While flipping a monster face-down resets many applied card effects and stat changes, the game state remembers that the physical card has already declared an attack this turn. Because no official database or web snippets were provided in the context, this is based on general rules knowledge; your on-site Head Judge is always authoritative. 

## Q11

**Question.** TCG ruling question. A player takes control of the opponent's monster during their Main Phase 1 (for example with Change of Heart). Can that player manually change the battle position of that monster that turn, if the monster was already on the field before the turn began? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** Yes, the player can manually change the battle position of the monster they took control of. Taking control of a monster does not count as Summoning or Setting it, so as long as the monster meets the standard conditions for a manual position change, the new controller is free to change it. 

## Q12

**Question.** TCG ruling question. A player controls 5 monsters in their Main Monster Zones and activates an effect that takes control of an opponent's monster (for example Change of Heart). Can they activate it? If the effect resolves and they have no free Main Monster Zone, what happens to the monster? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** You cannot activate "Change of Heart" if you do not have an available Main Monster Zone. If you activate it legally but your Main Monster Zones become full before the effect resolves, the targeted monster is destroyed by game mechanics and sent to the Graveyard. 

## Q13

**Question.** TCG ruling question. A player Normal Summons a monster into a Main Monster Zone. Before the Summon succeeds, could that chosen zone become occupied by another card (for example a card the opponent places on that player's field in response)? If a monster about to be Summoned has no zone left to go to, what happens to the Summon? Please cite official Konami sources.

**Answer (conclusion).** A chosen zone cannot become occupied by another card before a Normal Summon succeeds. Normal Summons do not start a Chain, meaning the opponent cannot activate cards or effects "in response" to the attempt to Summon; the monster is placed in the zone immediately upon declaration.
